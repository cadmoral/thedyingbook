At candlelight my reading becomes occlusive: I press the words against my chest so they cannot break free and leave this soul of mine that is giving birth to them. My tongue is still and I sense the burning burning eyes fixed on my trembling but silent lips as I peruse the manuscript, caressing its calligraphy with nothing but the light of this candle.

 

This is the regard that burnt aflame in Carthage and that found himself under the fig-tree as his eyes encountered the precise words and this is the regard that cried for millennia for not having been able to see earlier. Now I have become the book he tries and fails to decypher: my lips waver without sound, invoking private words that he cannot access.

 

But I am not looking at him: my attention is focused on this volume. Rags of putrid animals imprison wisdom, but beams of candlelight can open the lock of this door. Cross the threshold. Only at candlelight can I unwind the paths of this maze, in an internal dialogue with the unutterable.

 

Gleams of wax encircle my vision as I become aware that outside of this cell, back there in the dark illiterate ages, the incorporeal beam of knowledge that is penetrating me would turn into a mere piece of dead lamb skin. Wisdom, like the reading of this text, cannot be but labyrinthine: a tiresome descent into the inner treasures kept by these cold, meaningless walls.
